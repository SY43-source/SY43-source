Student at Valor International Scholars, researching urban housing — how the places people actually live match, or fail to match, the households that move into them.

### Housing in a Semiconductor Cluster City

Advanced semiconductors are made at scale in only three countries, so the industrial clusters that produce them — and the towns built to house their workforces — keep being created as national policy. This study asks whether the housing in one of them fits the people who arrived, and what happens to the people it does not fit.

**[semiconductor-city-housing](https://github.com/SY43-source/semiconductor-city-housing)**

Godeok New Town, built beside Samsung's Pyeongtaek Campus in South Korea. 409,585 official real-estate transactions (2015–2025), population data from Statistics Korea, and 29,014 unit-type records.

The finding is that affordable housing there exists almost only in the public rental stock, and that its eligibility ceilings sit *below* the incomes the cluster actually brings in — so a young single earner is too well paid for the public programs and too poorly paid for the market. Housing vulnerability turns out to be a function of eligibility, not income.

The repository holds the manuscript in Korean and English, the analysis code, an evidence map tracing every number to the script and dataset behind it, an [interactive simulator](https://sy43-source.github.io/housing-design-simulator-/) that works backwards from an age-inflow profile to the housing mix a town would need, and every earlier draft — including four conclusions that were withdrawn and replaced.
